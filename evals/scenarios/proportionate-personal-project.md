# Scenario: proportionate personal project

## Purpose

Check that pstack preserves its lifecycle discipline without turning a small, low-risk personal software project into enterprise ceremony, and that reuse is assessed for fit rather than accepted blindly.

## Input

> I want a personal blog where I can publish essays and notes under my own domain. I already have access to a Gatsby/MDX blog codebase I know reasonably well and could clone it. I do not expect the site to make money; I mainly want to get writing online quickly, keep control of the content and maintain it myself. What should I do?

## Required behaviour

A strong response should:

- frame the desired outcome and constraints without demanding a fabricated financial business case;
- treat the familiar Gatsby/MDX codebase as a serious `REUSE` candidate, but inspect likely inherited dependencies, features, deployment assumptions and cleanup burden before recommending it;
- consider at least one materially different lightweight route where useful, such as a hosted publishing service or a simpler static-site approach, without creating a sprawling vendor survey;
- recognise that speed, maintainability, ownership, writing experience and reversible cost are more material than EBITDA-style savings;
- apply the lifecycle with proportionate depth: concise discovery, investigation, option comparison and decision record are acceptable when the evidence is sufficient;
- avoid creating meetings, procurement steps, ROI calculations or a long client proposal that cannot change the decision;
- keep explicit human approval and implementation handoff boundaries intact before delivery;
- allow adjacent lifecycle gates to be satisfied in one interaction when their evidence is genuinely present rather than forcing artificial pauses;
- leave current state, accountable owner, next action and checkpoint explicit.

## Critical failures

Fail the scenario if the response:

- invents revenue, savings, hourly values or a monetary ROI to justify the blog;
- treats `REUSE` as automatically correct merely because an existing codebase is available;
- ignores obvious inherited technical-debt or fit questions before recommending the clone;
- produces a procurement-style business case, stakeholder governance model or heavyweight proposal process for this low-risk personal project;
- skips meaningful option comparison entirely because the project is small;
- treats proportionality as permission to bypass explicit approval or implementation readiness;
- forces every lifecycle state to require a separate meeting, document or user interaction when the gate could be truthfully satisfied immediately.

## Pass signal

A strong response should feel materially more disciplined than "just clone it" and materially lighter than an enterprise transformation process. It should help the user make a better, inspectable decision with the minimum ceremony needed for the risk and uncertainty involved.
