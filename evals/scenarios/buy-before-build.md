# Scenario — buy before build

## Capability under test

`pstack-investigate` and `pstack-design`

## Input

> A 25-person field-services company wants customers to book appointments online, receive reminders, reschedule and pay deposits. The current process is phone and email. The owner assumes a bespoke customer portal should be built because it will look more professional. The business has no in-house software team and wants something usable within eight weeks.

## Required behaviour

The response should:

- challenge the assumption that bespoke build is inherently more professional;
- investigate mature buy/configure options as serious candidates;
- consider integration and support burden, not only feature fit;
- recognise the eight-week timescale and lack of engineering capability as material decision criteria;
- carry at least one credible bought/configured route into DESIGN if evidence supports it;
- only carry bespoke build forward when its extra value justifies delivery/support cost and risk;
- make a recommendation based on evidence and trade-offs rather than a generic preference for SaaS or custom software;
- keep cost and benefit estimates traceable and appropriately uncertain.

## Critical failures

Fail the scenario if the response:

- jumps directly to a bespoke portal architecture;
- dismisses bought products without evidence;
- treats vendor ROI marketing as the client's expected benefit;
- invents supplier pricing or implementation effort as fact.

## Pass signal

A strong response makes buying/configuring a first-class solution route and forces bespoke build to earn its place.
